\section{Released data and code}
\label{sec:release}

Everything this paper reports can be regenerated from the release at \CodeURL{}, and the release
is also a resource in its own right: a fully adjudicated record of where three verifiers disagree on
a public leaderboard, with human labels.

\paragraph{What is released.}
(i) The verdict of each verifier, including xFinder, on every one of the \NumRows{} responses of the
\NumModels{} models, with the parse and time-out flags each verifier records; responses are identified by model and item identifier rather than copied.
(ii) The human labels: the 400-item audit (the answer the labeller found and the resulting reader
verdict) and the overlaps of \LabTwoN{} and \LabThreeN{} items labelled by two further labellers.
(iii) The labelling sheets, the offline labelling application and the rulebooks, including those of
the larger audits that were designed but not labelled, which enter no result.
(iv) The board records: for every model scored before the change of verifier, the results file
behind its displayed score and that file's history (Appendix~\ref{app:board}), and the census of
scoring rules in four evaluation frameworks.
(v) The code that computes every number, table and figure from these files, with its tests, and the
dated analysis plan.

\paragraph{Sources and licences.}
The problems and reference solutions are those of MATH-Hard as distributed for the leaderboard
(\texttt{lighteval/MATH-Hard}, MIT licence). The model responses come from the leaderboard's public
per-model details datasets on the Hugging Face Hub, whose dataset cards declare no licence; the
release therefore copies the text only of the responses shown to labellers, for research use, and
refers to every other response by model and item identifier, which match the public datasets. Our code, verdicts and labels are released under the Apache 2.0 licence.

\paragraph{Format, hosting and maintenance.}
Verdicts and labels are CSV and JSON files with one row per response. The release is hosted on
GitHub and maintained by the author, who will answer issues there; every change is recorded in the
repository's history, and the version that produced this paper is tagged
\texttt{dmlr-submission}. A response's verdicts depend on each verifier's version, and the release
pins the versions of the libraries it uses.

\paragraph{Responsible use.}
The labels describe the answers that model responses to competition mathematics problems give; they
contain no personal data and no information about the labellers. They measure what a careful reader
finds in a response, not whether a model followed the demonstrated output format, and they should not
be used to rank models outside the roster without a new audit.
