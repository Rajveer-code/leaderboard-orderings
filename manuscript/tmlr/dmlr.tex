\impact{Reporting which verifier produced a score, and how many responses it could not read, helps
readers, model developers and leaderboard maintainers tell a ranking difference that reflects the
models from one that reflects the scoring, and it costs a leaderboard almost nothing, because the
pipeline already computes that count. Two harms are possible. The finding that a change of verifier
reorders much of a board could be read as a reason to dismiss leaderboards altogether, although the
same analysis certifies many orderings; we report what is certified alongside what is not. And
knowing which verifier rewards which output format could help a developer tune a model's formatting
to a verifier rather than improve its answers; publishing the verifier and its failure count makes
such tuning visible rather than easier. The human labelling involved reading model responses to
competition mathematics problems; no personal data was collected.}
